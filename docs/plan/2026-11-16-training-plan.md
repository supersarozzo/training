## 1. Words you need (read once)

- **Rep** (repetition): one full movement, for example one lift of the weight.
- **Set**: a group of reps done without a break. "3 x 10" means 3 sets of 10 reps.
- **Rest**: the break between sets.
- **Superset (pair)**: two exercises done one after the other, then a rest. Example: A1, then A2, then rest, then A1 again.
- **RIR** (reps in reserve): how many more good reps you could still do when you stop. RIR 2 means you stop with 2 reps left in the tank.
- **Working set**: a real, hard set. A **warm-up set** is a light set before it.
- **Form**: how well you do the movement. Good form = slow, controlled, full range, no swinging.
- **Cardio**: exercise that raises your heart rate, such as a bike or treadmill.
- **Easy cardio ("talk pace")**: you can speak in short sentences. About 5 out of 10 effort.
- **Deload**: an easier week so your body can rest and grow.
- **Smallest step**: the smallest weight jump the gym allows (for example one dumbbell size up, or one plate on a machine).

## 2. The week

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Upper body A (chest, back, shoulders, arms) |
| Tuesday | Lower body A (legs, glutes) |
| Wednesday | Full body (a bit lighter) |
| Thursday | Upper body B |
| Friday | Lower body B |
| Saturday, Sunday | Rest. Walk if you like. |

**Why this split.** Each muscle gets trained about twice a week, which is good for building muscle. The two upper days and the two lower days are not back to back. Wednesday is a lighter full-body day, so the week does not wear you out. Saturday and Sunday give your body time to recover.

## 3. Every session (about 75 minutes)

| Block | Time |
|---|---|
| 1. Cardio start: easy warm-up | 8 min |
| 2. Weights: 3 pairs of exercises | about 40 min |
| 3. Abs | about 7 min |
| 4. Cardio end: the bigger cardio block | 15 min |
| Moving between machines, water, phone | about 5 min |

### Cardio: what you asked for and what I suggest (question 15)
You asked for a big cardio block at the start and at the end. For building muscle, long or hard cardio before weights makes you tired, so you lift less weight and build less muscle. So I suggest:
- **Start: 8 minutes of easy cardio.** It warms you up. It does not tire you.
- **End: 15 minutes of steady cardio.** Your legs and arms are tired by then, and the cardio does not hurt your lifting.
You still get cardio at both ends. Only the big part moves to the end. Other options are in question 15.

### Why pairs and not non-stop circuits (question 16)
A non-stop circuit keeps your heart rate high but gives you too little rest. Tired muscles lift lighter weights, and lighter weights build less muscle. Pairs fix this. Each pair uses two different muscles, so one muscle rests while the other works. You still get a sweat and save time, and you can lift heavy enough to grow.

### How to do a pair
1. Do set 1 of A1.
2. Walk to A2 (about 30 seconds). Do set 1 of A2.
3. Rest the time in the table ("Rest" column), then repeat until all sets are done.
4. Then move on to pair B, then pair C.

### Effort words used in the tables
- **Medium**: RIR 3 (you could do 3 more).
- **Hard**: RIR 2 (you could do 2 more).
- **Very hard**: RIR 1 (you could do 1 more). Never go to the point where you cannot do another rep, and never let your form break.
These are for weeks 2 to 5. See the 12-week view for other weeks.

## 5. Week 1: learn the moves and find your weights

Week 1 is not about hard work. It is about learning and writing down numbers.

**How to find a starting weight for each exercise**
1. Warm-up set 1: very light weight, 10 reps.
2. Warm-up set 2: about 70% of what you think you can use, 6 reps.
3. First working set: pick a weight. Do reps until you have 2 or 3 reps left (RIR 2-3), and stop. Stop before the top of the rep range if form gets worse.
4. Too easy (you reach the top of the rep range with 4 or more left)? Add the smallest step for the next set. Too hard (you cannot reach the bottom of the range)? Remove the smallest step.
5. Write down the weight you used. That is your starting weight for week 2.

**Week 1 rules**
- Do only 2 working sets per exercise (not 3). Skip the 3rd set.
- Stop every set with 2-3 reps left. It will feel too easy. That is on purpose.
- Warm-up sets before each exercise on your first day with it. Later in the same day, one light warm-up set only for the first exercise of each pair is enough.
- Cardio and abs as normal. Ask staff to show you the machines on day 1 if you are unsure. They are used to this.

## 6. Progression: when to add weight (the rule the app applies)

This is called **double progression**: first you add reps, then weight. Look at the rep range of the exercise (for example 8-12).

| What happened last session | What to do next session |
|---|---|
| Every working set reached the top of the range (for example 12) with good form, and you had 1 or more reps left | Add the **smallest step**. Go back to the bottom of the range. |
| Some sets are inside the range, but not all at the top | Keep the same weight. Try for 1 more rep per set. |
| One or more sets finished 1 or 2 reps below the bottom of the range (for example 6 or 7 when the range is 8-12) | Keep the same weight. Check rest and form. |
| One or more sets finished 3 or more reps below the bottom of the range (for example 5 or fewer when the range is 8-12), **two sessions in a row** | Lower the weight by about 10% (round down to a size the gym has). Restart at the bottom of the range. |
| Pain (not normal muscle burn), or form broke down | Lower the weight. Do not add weight until form is good. |

**Smallest steps:**
- Dumbbell exercises: the next dumbbell size up (usually 1 to 2 kg).
- Machines and cables: one plate on the stack (about 2.5 to 5 kg).
- Smith squat and hip thrust: 2.5 kg each side.
- If the smallest step is too big for the app user (the reps fall to the bottom of the range and stay there), keep the weight for another session. A step is fine to try for one session.

**Bodyweight exercises (plank, side plank, dead bug, reverse crunch):** when you reach the top of the range on all sets, add 5 seconds per set (planks) or 2 reps per set (the others).

**Cardio:** keep the same minutes. When talk pace feels too easy, raise speed or resistance a little.

**Week 12 check:** write down your weight, a body measurement (waist, chest, upper arm) and, if you like, a photo. Compare with week 1. After week 12, go back to week 2 with the new weights, or ask for a new plan.

**Deload weeks 6 and 12:** you do less on purpose. Cardio stays the same. If you feel great and rested, still follow the plan. The easier week is part of building muscle.

**Optional cardio change from week 7:** on Wednesday and Friday, in the 15-minute end block, swap minutes 5 to 12 for 3 rounds of "1 minute faster, 1 minute easy". Warm up and cool down as normal.

## 9. Form cues (2-3 lines each, in my own words)

**Machines and cables**
- **Chest press machine:** Set the seat so the handles are at mid-chest. Push forward without locking your elbows. Lower slowly, with your shoulder blades pulled back.
- **Shoulder press machine:** Set the seat so the handles are at ear level. Push up, keep your back on the pad. Lower slowly to the start.
- **Pulldown (high pulley):** Sit or kneel, pull the bar down to your upper chest, with elbows going down and back. Do not lean far back. Let the bar rise slowly.
- **Seated cable row:** Sit tall, feet on the plate or floor. Pull the handle to your belly, squeeze your shoulder blades. Let your arms stretch slowly, do not round your back.
- **Face pull:** Rope at face height. Pull the rope towards your face with elbows high and wide. Squeeze the back of your shoulders for 1 second.
- **Triceps pushdown:** Elbows tucked at your sides and still. Push down until your arms are straight, then let the handle rise slowly.
- **Overhead triceps extension (cable):** Face away from the cable, rope behind your head, elbows pointing forward and close. Straighten your arms forward and up, then lower slowly.
- **Leg press:** Feet shoulder-width on the plate. Lower until your knees are bent about 90 degrees, with your lower back flat on the seat. Push through your whole foot. Do not lock your knees at the top.
- **Leg extension:** Pad on your lower shins. Straighten your legs, pause for 1 second, then lower slowly. Do not swing.
- **Cable crunch:** Kneel under the high cable with the rope at your head. Curl your ribs towards your hips (do not pull with your arms). Return slowly.
- **Cable pull-through:** Face away from the low cable, rope between your legs. Push your hips back with soft knees, then squeeze your glutes to stand tall. It is a hip movement, not a squat.

**Dumbbells and bench**
- **One-arm dumbbell row:** One hand and one knee on the bench. Back flat. Pull the dumbbell to your hip, elbow close to your body. Lower slowly.
- **Dumbbell bench press:** Lie on the bench, feet on the floor. Lower the dumbbells to the sides of your chest, then press up and slightly together.
- **Incline dumbbell press:** Same as above on a bench with a slight slope (about 30 degrees). Keep your shoulder blades back.
- **Seated dumbbell shoulder press:** Back on the upright bench. Press the dumbbells up, do not arch your back. Lower to about ear level.
- **Dumbbell curl:** Elbows by your sides. Curl up, squeeze, lower slowly. Do not swing your body.
- **Hammer curl:** Same as the curl, with palms facing each other (like holding a hammer).
- **Lateral raise:** Light weight. Lift your arms out to the sides up to shoulder height with a small bend in the elbow. Lower slowly. Do not shrug.
- **Goblet squat:** Hold one dumbbell or kettlebell at your chest. Feet a bit wider than your hips. Sit down between your knees, chest up, then stand up.
- **Romanian deadlift:** Dumbbells in front of your thighs, soft knees. Push your hips back, slide the weights down your legs with a flat back until you feel a stretch behind your thighs. Stand up by pushing your hips forward.
- **Dumbbell step-up:** Place one foot fully on a low bench. Push through that heel to stand up. Lower slowly. Do all reps on one leg, then switch.

**Barbell, Smith and glute area**
- **Smith machine squat:** Bar on your upper back. Feet slightly in front of the bar. Sit down and back, knees in line with your toes, go down until thighs are about parallel with the floor, then stand up.
- **Hip thrust:** Upper back on the bench, bar or weight on your hips (use a pad). Push through your heels, squeeze your glutes at the top, ribs down. Lower slowly.
- **Standing calf raise:** Balls of your feet on a step or plate. Go all the way up, pause for 1 second. Go slowly all the way down for a good stretch.

**Abs and floor**
- **Plank:** Elbows under your shoulders, body in one straight line. Squeeze your glutes and tummy. Breathe normally. Stop if your lower back sags.
- **Side plank:** Lie on your side, one elbow under your shoulder, hips lifted. Keep your body in a straight line. Do both sides.
- **Dead bug:** Lie on your back, arms up, knees bent at 90 degrees. Slowly lower one arm and the opposite leg. Keep your lower back pressed to the floor. Switch sides.
- **Reverse crunch:** Lie on your back, knees bent. Use your abs to lift your hips slightly off the floor and curl your knees towards your chest. Lower slowly, do not swing.

## 10. Safety

- **Warm-up:** the 8-minute easy cardio, plus the light warm-up sets (week 1 explains how). Do not skip them.
- **Stop at once and get help if you feel:** sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, trouble breathing, or feel faint. Do not push through these.
- Normal muscle burn and soreness 1-2 days after training are fine. Joint pain is not.
- Drink water. Eat enough, and enough protein, to build muscle (a separate topic; not covered here).
- Use the safety stops on the Smith machine. Ask a staff member if you are not sure how a machine works.
- **This is not medical advice.** If you have a health worry, talk to a doctor first.

## 11. Notes for the app (later)

**What the app must show per day:** the block list in order (cardio start, weights pairs, abs, cardio end), with the exercise name, equipment, target sets and reps, rest, effort word, the swap, and the form cue (in a tap-to-open panel).

**Timers**
- Cardio start: countdown 8 min (5 min in short mode). Cardio end: countdown 15 min (10 min in short mode). Optional interval timer from week 7 (1 min fast, 1 min easy x 3).
- Rest timer: starts when the user marks a set as done. A pair uses a 30 s timer after A1 and the pair's rest after A2.
- Plank and side plank: a count-up or countdown timer for the target seconds.
- Keep the screen on during the session. A web app cannot vibrate the phone, and sound may not play with the screen locked, so the timer must be visible.

**What to log per set:** date, week number, day, exercise, set number, weight (kg), reps (or seconds), how hard it felt (RIR 0-4+, optional), a note. For cardio: machine, minutes, optional distance. For the check in week 12: bodyweight and measurements.

**Data the app needs to apply the progression rule:** per exercise: rep range, smallest step (kg), last session's reps per set, count of "3 or more below the bottom" sessions in a row, the current week's phase (learn, build, deload, push). Prefer to show the next weight as a suggestion the user can change.

**Modes:** a "short session (60 min)" switch (section 8). A "machine busy" button that shows the swap and logs the swap used.
