# Walk check: is the treadmill walk right?

Date: 2026-11-18. Written by a research helper. Rosario asked: "Is it right to do the walk? Is it right that many minutes? That speed and that uphill angle?"

Privacy: this file has no body numbers and no private speed or slope numbers. "Your starting row" means the row in `private/plan-v2-personal.md`.

## 1. Short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Do the walk before weights? | **Yes, keep it.** Walking first does not stop muscle growth. Doing weights first gives, at most, a little more leg strength. |
| 30 minutes (3 build-up + 27 fast)? | **Yes, keep it.** 5 days of 30 minutes is 150 minutes a week. That is the standard minimum, and it is on the low side for fat loss, not too much. |
| Speed and slope? | **Yes for the start and the rules. One change to think about: the top limits.** Your starting row is moderate. The top limit (12 % and 6 km/h together) is very hard, much harder than "talk in short sentences". The effort rule protects you, so I suggest a lower cap (section 4). |

## 2. The evidence

**A. Walking before lifting is fine for muscle.**
- A big review of 43 studies found that adding cardio to weight training did not reduce muscle size or maximal strength. Only explosive power (jumping, sprinting) dropped, and mostly when cardio and weights were in the same session. Source: Schumann et al. 2022, Sports Medicine, via [JCU repository](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/71546/) and [sci-sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/concurrent-training-does-cardio-affect-muscle-mass-and-strength-gains-233/).
- A review of exercise order found no order effect on muscle size or body fat. It found a small benefit of weights first for leg strength only. Source: Eddens et al. 2018, Sports Medicine, [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s40279-017-0784-1) and [Northumbria](https://nrl.northumbria.ac.uk/id/eprint/31702).
- Caution: one study shows tiredness right after hard cardio can lower the next lift in the same session ([PubMed 34878640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34878640/)). This is why the plan keeps the walk "talk in short sentences", with 3 minutes of rest before the first heavy set.
- Caution 2: a smaller review of muscle fibres found a small negative effect, a bit more with running than with cycling ([sci-sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/concurrent-training-does-cardio-affect-muscle-mass-and-strength-gains-233/)). Walking is gentler than running, so the risk is lower. I found no study of incline walking alone.

**B. Minutes per week.**
- American Heart Association (2026 statement): at least 150 minutes a week of moderate cardio plus weights on 2 or more days. For fat loss and keeping it off, about 200 to 300 minutes a week, and at least 225 to 420 minutes for larger loss from exercise alone ([AHA statement highlights](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/move-more-for-your-health-not-just-for-the-scale), [EurekAlert](https://e3.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1129529)).
- ACSM position paper (2009): under 150 minutes a week gave little weight loss; 225 to 420 minutes gave about 5 to 7.5 kg ([NCBI Bookshelf NBK565813](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK565813/)).
- Meaning: 150 minutes of walking plus 5 weight days is a solid base. Diet matters more than extra cardio, and 30 minutes is a good length because the 5 weight sessions also use energy and need recovery. Weights (not cardio) are what keeps muscle during fat loss ([AHA statement](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/move-more-for-your-health-not-just-for-the-scale)).

**C. Slope and speed: energy and effort.**
- Walking on a slope costs much more energy: about 23 % more at 10 % slope and about 44 % more at 16 %, compared with flat (older adults, [PMC8383420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8383420/)). The calf muscles (soleus) matched the extra cost most. Another study in 16 adults at 0, 5 and 10 % shows the same rise ([PubMed 22578744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22578744/)). So slope is an efficient way to raise effort without running.
- "12-3-30" (12 % slope, 3 mph = about 4.8 km/h, 30 minutes): no trial has tested it as a package. Experts say it is good, low-impact cardio with no special magic, and not a starting point for people who are not already active; it burns roughly 250 to 350 kcal for most adults, not 500 ([Healthline experts](https://www.healthline.com/health-news/what-experts-think-of-the-12-3-30-tiktok-treadmill-workout), [Acibadem summary](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/the-12-3-30-workout-what-the-viral-treadmill-routine-does-and-who-should-modify-it/)). Our plan already treats it as a later target, not a start. Good.
- My own check with the standard ACSM walking formula (valid up to about 6 km/h): 12 % slope at 4.8 km/h is about 8 METs (a "vigorous" level). 12 % at 6 km/h is about 10 METs. That is far above "talk in short sentences", which is about 4 to 6 METs. So the top corner of the app's range is a hard workout, not a warm-up.

**D. Joints, calves, shins, back, rails.**
- Calves and Achilles: in trained runners, the Achilles adapted to uphill and downhill running; the main risk was adding load too fast ([News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20150216/Achilles-tendon-can-adapt-to-uphill-and-downhill-running-better-than-previously-thought-Study.aspx), [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/releases/289451)). I found no study on incline walking and the Achilles. This is running evidence in fit people: treat it as a hint only.
- Shins: a review notes uphill running loads the shin bone and lower leg muscles more ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1505263/pdf)). Evidence for walking is thin. Slow steps (1 % at a time) are the right protection.
- Rails: holding the rails and leaning back cut the energy cost by about a third in one study (10 % slope). Holding while standing upright did not ([digitalcommons.wku.edu IJES](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol7/iss4/10); [front-panel support study](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijesab/vol9/iss2/31)). So leaning on the rails makes the walk easier than the numbers say and bends the lower back. The plan's rule (touch for balance only) is right.
- Lower back: I found no solid study. Standing tall and short steps is common coaching advice, not tested evidence.

**E. Warm-up value.**
- Dynamic (moving) warm-ups have fair evidence for better strength and power in the session ([BJSM review 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25694615/)). Evidence that they cut injuries is thin ([Sports Medicine 2023 review](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40279-023-01847-8.pdf)). Both sources also say very long warm-ups can hurt performance.
- Meaning: the walk is a good general warm-up (heart, legs). The 4-minute joint warm-up and the light warm-up sets add the upper-body and joint part. Keeping both is reasonable. I found no review that directly compares an aerobic warm-up with a dynamic one before lifting, so this part rests on coaching practice.

## 4. Recommended changes

1. **Keep the walk, 30 minutes, and leg-day rule as they are.** The evidence supports them.
2. **Lower the top cap (my main suggestion).** 12 % and 6 km/h together is about a vigorous level, and no evidence supports it for a warm-up before heavy legs. Options: (a) keep 12 % slope but cap speed near 5 km/h, close to the 12-3-30 walk; or (b) keep the caps and rely on the effort rule (effort 5 to 6 only). I prefer (a), because effort questions are answered by feel and can drift. This is a plan decision for Rosario, not a bug.
3. **Reaching the cap soon is not a goal.** Calves are already worked 3 days a week by weights plus the walk (plan section 17). Slow steps are fine. If calves or shins hurt, hold the slope for a week.
4. **Optional text for the screen:** one line saying "Touch the rails lightly. Leaning back makes the walk easier and can hurt your back."

**Should Rosario be able to change the walk's minutes, speed and slope in the app?**

| | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Change before the walk (a setting or "adjust" button) | Real days differ: tired legs, a different treadmill, a sore calf. The treadmill speed buttons may not match 0.2 steps. They will change them anyway, and it is better to record it. | More choices on a phone in a gym. Easy to drift upward. The progress rule then follows the changed numbers. |
| Change after the walk (exists today) | Records what really happened; the next suggestion stays honest. | They must remember. |
| Change minutes | Useful when time is short (a short-day mode already exists). | Weekly walk minutes drop below the 150 base. Keep it as "shorter today", not a new default. |

My view: **yes, allow a small change before the walk**, within limits (slope plus or minus 2 %, speed plus or minus 0.5 km/h, minutes 10 to 30), and show "your change" next to "plan's pick". Keep after-walk saving as today. Do not let the app raise the plan caps by itself. This is a design choice for Rosario.
