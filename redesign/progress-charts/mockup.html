⚠️ Where I disagree with you

  1. A daily calories chart can make every day feel like a test. One high day does not change your weight; the week does. So the big number on the card is the 7-day average (the same number Diet already shows), not today, and a day over the target has the same colour as any other day: no red, no warning.
  2. You asked for a body weight graph, but it already exists. Adding a second one would split your weigh-ins. NEW keeps one chart and fixes why it was easy to miss (empty card, small title, too far down).
  3. Daily weigh-ins jump up and down by up to 1 kg (water, salt, food in the gut). The line is the 7-day trend, and that is the number to watch. NEW adds one line of words under the chart that says so.

How well-made apps do it

From my knowledge of these apps, not opened again today (see Not verified).

  • Apple Health (Dietary Energy): one bar per day, the average as the big number above the chart, D / W / M / 6M / Y switch. Days with no data have no bar.
  • MyFitnessPal (Nutrition, weekly): bars per day with the daily goal as a line, net average under it.
  • Lose It!: a week of bars against the daily budget; under-budget and over-budget days in different colours (we do not copy the colours: see "Where I disagree", point 1).
  • MacroFactor and Happy Scale (weight): each weigh-in as a small dot and a smooth trend line on top. This is the same idea as our weight chart.
  • Cronometer: calorie history as bars with a target line; days not logged are left empty.
  • Food search with a built-in list (Cronometer, MyFitnessPal, Apple-style search): plain "generic" foods from national food tables come first or next to your own foods, branded products after. Our NEW search does the same with the UK tables.
  • The UK food tables: gov.uk page "Composition of foods integrated dataset (CoFID)", read 18 Nov 2026: Excel file 4.42 MB, Open Government Licence v3.0, last updated 19 March 2021.

Where NEW differs from the live app

  • Weight card: title "Body weight" as a card title (h2, 20 px; the live h2 is 21 px) instead of the eyebrow; the date as "18 Nov"; the trend number in h2 size (20 px, the live app uses 30 px, not in DESIGN.md); one line of words under the chart.
  • Weight chart: 3 grid lines (80.5, 81.5, 82.5 kg) instead of 5, so the line uses the whole height; line 2 px. With answer 102 a: no glow, no shaded area. New scale rule, Rosario to approve: at most 4 lines, steps of 0.5, 1, 2 or 5 (times 10, 100...), and the first line may sit on a half step when that fits the weigh-ins in fewer lines.
  • Units in small muted text next to the number ("80.8" big, "kg" small), as DESIGN.md section 4 says; the live app writes "80.8 kg" all big.
  • "Open Diet" button on the Calories card (secondary button, like "Weigh in" on the weight card), Rosario to approve. It is a second button on Progress; without it, the way to log food is the Diet tab.
  • "Average a day" is the same number as Diet's "Last 7 days, average" (the 7 days before today, days with food only), so the two screens never disagree.
  • Kept from the live app although DESIGN.md says otherwise (out of scope, listed for later): the secondary button (18 px, weight 650, surface fill and soft border; DESIGN.md section 8 says a hairline in border-strong and no fill), the Settings pill and the switch labels (15 px), the eyebrow (13 px; DESIGN.md says 12 px), the totals numbers (24 px).
  • New calories card under the weight card, then the workout totals (answer 103 a).
  • Not changed and kept on purpose: the page title (live 32 px; DESIGN.md says 26 to 28 px), the totals tiles (24 px numbers), the exercise list and recent workouts. They are out of scope for this step; listed as later work.
